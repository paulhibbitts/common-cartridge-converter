Convert a Common Cartridge (.imscc) export to a Grav pages folder for Grav Helios Course Hub

Have full control of your course content. Grav Helios Course Hub stores content in portable open Markdown files — no database, no vendor lock-in. You can also embed any page directly into Canvas, Moodle, or Brightspace with a simple URL parameter, and use your LMS as a partner for assignments and grading while keeping full access to your content. This converter gets you there from a Common Cartridge export.

  1. 1
    In Canvas, go to Settings → Export Course Content and choose Common Cartridge 1.1. In other LMS platforms (Moodle, D2L, Blackboard), use their equivalent course export and select Common Cartridge format. Download the .imscc file.
  2. 2
    Upload that file below and click Convert & Download ZIP. A ZIP containing a pages/{course-slug}/ folder ready for Grav will be downloaded.
  3. 3
    Extract the ZIP and copy the pages/{course-slug}/ folder into the user/pages/ directory of your Grav Helios Course Hub installation.
  4. 4
    Review the included conversion-notes.txt for a summary of what was converted, what was not included, and any warnings to address.

What exactly does this do?

It's a self-contained PHP web app running on the server. You upload a Common Cartridge .imscc export, it parses the module structure and converts wiki page content to Markdown, then hands you back a ZIP ready to drop into Grav.

What's converted?

  1. Course modules → chapter landing pages with numbered child pages
  2. Wiki pages → course-page.md Markdown files with YAML frontmatter
  3. Module section headers (ContextModuleSubHeader) → group: frontmatter for visual grouping in the Course Hub sidebar
  4. Vimeo and YouTube iframes → [iframe] and [youtube] shortcodes
  5. A module.md card-grid listing page for all modules

What's not included?
Quizzes, discussions, and assignments have no equivalent in Helios Course Hub and are not included. A full count appears in conversion-notes.txt in the ZIP.

What happens to your file?
The .imscc is extracted to a server temp directory, parsed, converted, and packaged. The temp files are immediately deleted after the ZIP is streamed to your browser — nothing is stored on the server.
